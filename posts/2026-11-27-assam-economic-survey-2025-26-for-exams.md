**Assam's 2025–26 Economic Survey puts the state's GSDP at ₹7,45,293 crore at current prices and ₹3,84,433 crore at constant 2011–12 prices.** Both are advance estimates for 2025–26. Use the same period, price basis and estimate type whenever you cite or compare the figures.

## Figures to know for Assam exams

| Assam measure for 2025–26 | Advance estimate | Where to check |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| GSDP at current prices | ₹7,45,293 crore | Chapter 3, advance-estimates box and section 3.1 |
| GSDP at constant 2011–12 prices | ₹3,84,433 crore | Chapter 3, advance-estimates box and section 3.2 |
| GSDP growth at current prices | 15.84% | Chapter 3, advance-estimates discussion |
| GSDP growth at constant 2011–12 prices | 10.12% | Chapter 3, advance-estimates discussion |
| Per capita income at current prices | ₹1,85,429 | Table 3.10 |
| Per capita income at constant 2011–12 prices | ₹93,351 | Table 3.10 |

The advance estimates apply to 2025–26. The chapter compares them with quick estimates for 2024–25. The same chapter includes a separate 2026–27 projection. Keep projections and estimates for different years on separate lines in your notes.

## Three quick practice questions

1. **Which figure gives Assam's real GSDP growth in the 2025–26 estimates?** It is **10.12%**, using constant 2011–12 prices.
2. **What is the survey's current-price estimate of Assam's per capita income?** **₹1,85,429**. The constant-price entry sits in another column of the same table.
3. **How does the survey derive per capita income?** It divides net state domestic product by the state's mid-year population. Section 3.8 defines it.

## Use the evidence in an APSC Mains answer

**Practice prompt:** Why should an assessment of Assam's recent growth cite both nominal and real measures?

Open with the comparison: “The Economic Survey's 2025–26 advance estimates put Assam's nominal GSDP growth at 15.84% and real growth at 10.12%.” Explain that the constant-price series holds the price base fixed and helps assess changes in output. For a question about living standards, pair the growth comparison with per capita income and acknowledge that an average alone does not show how gains are distributed.

## Frequently asked questions

### What does AE mean in the Assam Economic Survey?

AE means advance estimate. The cited figures use that label for the 2025–26 financial year.

### Which number should I quote for real growth?

Use the constant-price GSDP growth estimate. For 2025–26, the chapter's advance-estimates discussion gives 10.12%.

### Is per capita income the same as GSDP divided by population?

The survey derives per capita income from **net** state domestic product divided by the mid-year population. Check section 3.8 and Table 3.10.
