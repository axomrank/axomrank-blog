For Assam exams, learn the order **Varman → Mlechchha (Salastambha) → Pala**. Connect each name to a ruler or source. The copper plates of **Bhaskaravarman** give you a useful starting point for the Varman period.

## A short revision table

| Dynasty | What to remember | Source to consult |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Varman | Pushyavarman is associated with the founding line. Bhaskaravarman is linked with Xuanzang's visit and the Nidhanpur plates. | Government museum record for the plates and Kamrup's district history |
| Mlechchha | The Salastambha line followed the Varmans. Sonitpur's district history discusses Harjaravarman's inscription. | Sonitpur district history |
| Pala | The Kamarupa Palas followed the Salastambha line. | Edward Gait's account of early Assam and the GMDA historical synopsis |

Xuanzang also appears in exam material as *Hiuen Tsang*. The National Mission on Monuments and Antiquities attributes both the Dubi and Nidhanpur copper plates to Bhaskaravarman.

## Frequently asked questions

### What was the first of these three Kamarupa dynasties?

The Varman dynasty comes first, followed by the Salastambha or Mlechchha line and then the Palas.

### Which ruler issued the Nidhanpur copper plates?

The National Mission on Monuments and Antiquities attributes the Nidhanpur plates to Bhaskaravarman.

### Which Kamarupa ruler is linked to Xuanzang's visit?

Bhaskaravarman was the ruler associated with Xuanzang's account of Kamarupa.
