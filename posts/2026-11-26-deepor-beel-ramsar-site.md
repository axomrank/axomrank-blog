**Deepor Beel is a freshwater wetland southwest of Guwahati.** It lies in a former channel of the Brahmaputra. The Ramsar Sites Information Service records its designation on 19 August 2002 as site number 1207, with an area of 4,000 hectares.

## Facts to remember for Assam exams

| What the question asks | Answer to remember |
| --- | --- |
| Location | Southwest of Guwahati, south of the present Brahmaputra channel |
| Wetland type | A permanent freshwater lake and wetland |
| Ramsar designation | 19 August 2002, site number 1207 |
| Area in the Ramsar record | 4,000 hectares |
| Why the wetland matters | Its freshwater habitat supports waterbirds and fisheries. The Ramsar record also describes a role in storing stormwater for Guwahati. |

Study the ecological function alongside the date. An environment question may ask what the beel does, while a geography question may ask where it lies.

## Frequently asked questions

### Where is Deepor Beel?

It is southwest of Guwahati in Assam, in a former channel of the Brahmaputra.

### When was Deepor Beel designated a Ramsar site?

The Ramsar record gives 19 August 2002. Its site number is 1207.

### Why is Deepor Beel important?

It provides wetland habitat for birds and fish and serves a stormwater storage role for Guwahati, as documented in the Ramsar information sheet.
